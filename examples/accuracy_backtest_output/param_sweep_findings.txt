Kronos-small Sampling Hyperparameter Sweep Findings
====================================================
Run date: 2026-07-28
Model: NeoQuasar/Kronos-small (tokenizer: NeoQuasar/Kronos-Tokenizer-base)
Script: examples/sweep_kronos_params.py
Granularity: Daily (BTCUSDT), context_len=90, block_len=5, 36 walk-forward
             blocks over the most recent ~182 days -- same config as the
             daily row in examples/accuracy_backtest_output/findings.txt,
             chosen so results are directly comparable to that baseline.
Wall-clock time: ~14 minutes total (model load + data fetch + 49 combos +
                 a follow-up reproducibility check), well under the 1-hour
                 budget. Per-combo cost on CPU (12 cores, torch using 6
                 threads): ~6s at sample_count=1, ~19s at sample_count=3,
                 ~34s at sample_count=5, for 36 batched blocks.

---------------------------------------------------------
1. GRID TESTED
---------------------------------------------------------
Stage 1 (coarse, 24 combos):
  T:             {0.6, 0.8, 1.0, 1.2}
  top_p:         {0.8, 0.9, 1.0}
  sample_count:  {1, 3}

Stage 2 (refinement around the stage-1 leader, 25 combos):
  T:             {0.9, 1.0, 1.1}   (+/-0.1 around best-so-far)
  top_p:         {0.85, 0.9, 0.95} (+/-0.05 around best-so-far)
  sample_count:  {1, 3, 5}

Total: 49 unique combos. Full per-combo results (MAE, RMSE, MAPE,
DirectionalAccuracy, Correlation, elapsed_sec) are in
param_sweep_results.csv in this directory.

---------------------------------------------------------
2. HEADLINE RESULT: NO COMBO RELIABLY BEATS A COIN FLIP
---------------------------------------------------------
Across all 49 combos:
  DirectionalAccuracy: mean=47.01%, std=4.00pp, min=35.42%, max=54.17%

The theoretical standard deviation of a 50%-coin-flip proportion estimated
from n=144 independent bar-direction calls (36 blocks x 4 within-block
diffs each, exactly how compute_metrics() computes it) is
sqrt(0.25/144) = 4.16 percentage points -- essentially identical to the
4.00pp spread actually observed across 49 different (T, top_p,
sample_count) combos. In other words, the entire 35-54% range seen in
this sweep is consistent with pure sampling noise around 50%, not a real
effect of the hyperparameters being varied.

Breaking down by sample_count alone confirms this -- no monotonic or
otherwise meaningful trend:
  sample_count=1: mean=46.88%, std=2.85pp (n=20)
  sample_count=3: mean=47.43%, std=5.31pp (n=20)
  sample_count=5: mean=46.37%, std=2.96pp (n=9)
Averaging more sampled paths (higher sample_count) did not systematically
improve directional accuracy; if anything it added variance (sc=3 has the
widest spread) without moving the mean.

DirectionalAccuracy vs. MAE across combos: correlation = 0.18 (weak) --
the combos with the "best" directional accuracy are not reliably the
combos with the best price-level error either, another sign these are
independent noisy quantities rather than a coherent quality axis.

---------------------------------------------------------
3. REPRODUCIBILITY CHECK ON THE "WINNING" COMBO
---------------------------------------------------------
The single best result in the whole sweep was T=0.9, top_p=0.85,
sample_count=3 -> DirAcc=54.17%, MAE=3088.2, Corr=0.788. Since Kronos's
autoregressive sampling is stochastic and NOT seeded between runs, this
result was re-run 3 more times with the identical parameters to check
whether it reproduces:

  T=0.9, top_p=0.85, sample_count=3 (3 fresh repeats):
    rep1: DirAcc=45.14%  rep2: DirAcc=43.06%  rep3: DirAcc=40.97%

For comparison, the baseline (T=1.0, top_p=0.9, sample_count=1) was also
re-run 3 times:
  rep1: DirAcc=43.06%  rep2: DirAcc=49.31%  rep3: DirAcc=43.75%

The "winning" combo's repeats (41-45%) are no better than -- and if
anything slightly worse than -- the baseline's repeats (43-49%). The
54.17% result that stood out in the sweep table did not reproduce at all;
it was a favorable random draw, not a real property of that parameter
combination. This directly confirms the noise-floor argument in section 2
using a completely independent test.

---------------------------------------------------------
4. RECOMMENDATION
---------------------------------------------------------
Do not change the current defaults (T=1.0, top_p=0.9, sample_count=1).
No combination of T in [0.6, 1.2], top_p in [0.8, 1.0], or sample_count
in [1, 5] produced a directional-accuracy improvement over baseline that
survived a basic reproducibility check. Raising sample_count from 1 to 3
or 5 multiplies compute cost (~3x / ~5x) for no measurable directional
benefit, so it is not worth the extra CPU time on this task.

Honest bottom line: directional accuracy for Kronos-small on daily
BTC/USDT next-bar prediction never meaningfully cleared ~50% under any
sampling hyperparameters tested. This is consistent with, and reinforces,
the original findings.txt conclusion -- the ~49-51% directional accuracy
ceiling looks like a property of the model/task (next-bar direction on
this series is close to unpredictable from Kronos-small's own confidence
distribution), not an artifact of poorly-tuned softmax temperature,
nucleus sampling threshold, or sample averaging. Any further attempt to
find directional edge should look elsewhere -- e.g. a larger model
(Kronos-base/large), longer prediction horizons where trend rather than
next-bar direction matters, additional input features, or accepting that
next-bar directional trading on this model/asset/granularity is not
viable and reframing the strategy around price-level tracking instead.
