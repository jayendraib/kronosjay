Indicator-Filtered + Hysteresis Kronos Strategy — Recent Month vs. Down-Trend Month
=====================================================================================
Script: examples/pnl_backtest_filtered.py
Model: Kronos-small predictions, unchanged from the original run.
Windows tested (each run as its own fresh $10,000 backtest):
  - "Recent": most recent 30 days of data (an UPTREND -- buy & hold
    roughly +4.6% to +5.7% depending on granularity).
  - "Down-Trend (Jun 2026)": 2026-06-01 to 2026-06-30, a clear
    down-trending month in this same BTC/USDT history (buy & hold
    roughly -17.9% to -20.7% depending on granularity).

Filter logic (unchanged from the first version, no retraining, pure
post-processing on Kronos's existing predictions):
  - Trend filter: actual close vs. its own 20-period SMA.
  - Momentum filter: MACD(12,26,9) histogram sign.
  - Magnitude filter: skip predicted moves smaller than 0.05%.
  A trade is only considered when Kronos's directional call agrees with
  BOTH filters and clears the magnitude threshold.

NEW — hysteresis: the first version of this filter was found to
*increase* trade count at 15-minute granularity (131 -> 393) because the
position kept flipping in/out of flat as the agree/disagree check
flickered bar to bar. Fixed with a debounce: once a non-flat position is
opened, it must be held for at least MIN_HOLD_BARS=3 bars before the
filter is allowed to change it again (see `apply_hysteresis`).

---------------------------------------------------------
TAKEAWAYS
---------------------------------------------------------
1. Hysteresis helped, but only partially fixed the 15-min churn problem.
   Recent-window 15-min trade count dropped from 393 (no hysteresis) to
   338 (with hysteresis) -- an improvement, but still well above the
   unfiltered baseline's 131 trades. A 3-bar (45-minute) minimum hold is
   too short at 15-min granularity to stop most of the flip-flopping;
   daily and hourly, where 3 bars is a more meaningful chunk of time (3
   days / 3 hours), show cleaner trade-count reductions (10->9 daily,
   93->74 hourly in the recent window).

2. The strategy's real value showed up in the down-trend month, not the
   uptrend one. In June's crash, Filtered+Hysteresis dramatically
   outperformed buy & hold at low slippage across every granularity:
   daily -1.5% vs. buy&hold -17.9%, hourly -0.25% vs. -20.65%, 15-min
   -7.56% vs. -20.57%. This makes sense -- the filter's trend/momentum
   agreement requirement naturally keeps the strategy flat or short
   during a sustained decline, which is exactly when "just holding BTC"
   hurts most. This is the clearest evidence yet that the filter adds
   real value: capital protection in adverse regimes, even though
   Kronos's raw directional accuracy hasn't changed.

3. Higher slippage still erodes the gains, especially at high trade
   frequency. At 0.54% slippage, the down-trend hourly result flips from
   beating buy & hold (-0.25%) to trailing it (-28.56%), and 15-min goes
   from -7.56% to -70.59%. The strategy is genuinely useful at
   realistic/low slippage but is not robust to a high-cost execution
   environment, particularly at 15-minute granularity where trade count
   is still elevated.

4. The uptrend month remains the weak spot: Filtered+Hysteresis beat
   unfiltered everywhere, and beat buy & hold at daily/hourly and low
   slippage, but never caught up to buy & hold at 15-min, and lost to it
   at high slippage everywhere except daily. A strategy that spends most
   of its time flat by design will structurally lag a strong sustained
   uptrend -- that's an expected, acceptable tradeoff if the goal is
   downside protection, not a flaw to "fix."

Recommended next steps if pursuing this further:
  - Increase MIN_HOLD_BARS specifically for 15-min (e.g. 8-12 bars ~ 2-3
    hours) rather than using one global constant across all
    granularities -- the current fix under-hilds at high frequency.
  - Test a sideways/choppy month too, to see how the filter behaves when
    there's no strong trend in either direction (the two windows tested
    so far are both trending, just in opposite directions).
  - Consider position sizing by signal strength instead of a binary
    trade/no-trade call, especially to reduce the 15-min churn without
    just brute-forcing a longer hold period.
